# 0003 — Always-on missile seeker on the nose axis, and assisted flight with a bounded gun aim assist on touch

- **Status**: accepted
- **Date**: 2026-10-10 (records decisions taken during the iPad controls and weapons round, which started from `5b4e1ac`: touch stick and assisted flight `6384e8f`; seeker, reticle and tap to fire `659fdf3`; continuous gun aim assist `214fcd1`, `db9bb72`; merged to `main` in `601def1`)

## Context

- The owner played on an iPad and reported that the touch controls felt odd and that missile lock-on was broken — it was hard to get a lock at the start of a level — and asked for weapons that are clearer, more fun and easier to pick up.
- Missile lock was a mode (state at `5b4e1ac`). A press of the missile key called `LockOnIndicator.startLockOn()`, and only then was anything drawn: a search ring fixed at the centre of the viewport. Progress built while a target stayed inside the ring; a target that left it for a single frame was dropped, and the next one started from zero. A completed lock launched the missile by itself 0.2 s later, and the player could not call that off. There was no gun reticle in either view.
- Measured in a real browser at 1180×820: in the third-person view the nose, and with it the gun line, projects about 170 px above the centre of the screen, while the ring's radius was 90 px (74 px with the touch layout). With the nose held on a target and the missile key down for five to six seconds the indicator never left `search`. In first person, where the camera looks along the nose, the same flow worked.
- The touch path was audited at the same time. The stick looked analogue but was thresholded into four booleans, so a turn was either full rate or nothing; it dropped the thumb as soon as it slid outside its 108 px box; and touch had no roll control and no levelling while the stick was deflected, so bank built up until pushing right no longer turned the aircraft right.
- For the guns the round planned a lead marker and a light aim assist on touch. The owner then added a request: the reticle itself should shift slightly, within a limit, with the assist.
- Keyboard flight was not part of the report and was to stay as it was.

## Decision

1. **An always-on seeker centred on the nose axis.** The aim point is the point 600 m ahead on the nose axis (`GAME_CONSTANTS.GUN_ASSIST.REFERENCE_RANGE`), projected through the active camera (`LockOnIndicator.computeAim`). A gun cross is drawn there in both views, placed again on every render frame, and the missile acquire ring is centred on it — on where the nose points, not on the centre of the screen.
   - **Always searching.** `MissileSeeker` (`src/features/combat/MissileSeeker.ts`, pure logic with no DOM) runs on every simulation step while a missile is aboard; there is no step that starts a lock. It tracks the candidate nearest the aim point inside the acquire ring, whose radius is 13% of the viewport's short side times the lock-radius upgrade, by one rule on every layout.
   - **Hold ring, grace and decay instead of a reset.** Progress grows only inside the acquire ring. Between it and a keep ring 1.6 times as large, progress drains (a full bar in one second) and a completed lock is held. Outside the keep ring, behind the camera or beyond 1,200 m, the target is given up only after 0.5 s. A target that is no longer in the candidate list (destroyed, recycled, cloaked) is dropped at once. Another target takes over only after a dwell (0.25 s while tracking, 0.6 s once locked) and, while the current one is still inside the ring, only if it is clearly closer to the reticle; it starts from zero.
   - **Tap to fire.** `GameCoordinator.handleMissileInput` acts on the press. With a lock, one missile leaves at once and the lock is kept, so the next press — at least 0.35 s later — fires again. A press made while the lock is still building fires nothing by itself; held, it fires once at the moment the lock completes. With nothing tracked, or released before the lock, it gives a `NO LOCK` cue. Nothing launches without a press.
2. **Assisted flight and a bounded gun aim assist on touch.**
   - **Analogue floating stick.** `InputState` carries `pitchAxis` / `yawAxis` (−1..1) next to the boolean directions, which stay filled in for existing readers. The stick's origin is wherever the thumb lands in `#touch-stick-zone`, and its deflection passes through a radial dead zone (0.12) and an exponential curve (1.7) (`TOUCH_STICK_TUNING`, `shapeStickMagnitude`). Keys map to ±1.
   - **An assisted model for the stick only.** `InputState.flightAssist` is true while a finger is on the stick and no steering key is down. `PlayerController.applyAssistedAttitude` then yaws about the world vertical, pitches about the level right axis — held to 75° above or below the horizon — and steers bank by itself: up to 45° into the turn at full deflection, back to wings level when the stick centres. Pushing right always turns right relative to the horizon. Keyboard steering, including a keyboard attached to a tablet, keeps the manual model (`applyManualAttitude`) unchanged.
   - **A bounded gun aim assist.** `GunLeadSolver` (`src/features/combat/GunLeadSolver.ts`, pure logic) solves a lead point from each target's estimated velocity and, on every device, marks it for the nearest hostile aircraft ahead. Where the assist is enabled it also returns one assisted direction, used by both the bullets (`PlayerSystem.setGunAimAssist`) and the gun cross. That direction points at the aim point while it lies within 2.5° of the nose (`GUN_ASSIST.FULL_ANGLE`), fades smoothly to nothing by 5° (`OUTER_ANGLE`), never shifts the aim more than 2.5° off the nose, and does nothing beyond 480 m. A change of assist target is eased over about 0.1 s, so the cross slides instead of jumping. While shifted, the cross takes the lock colour and a dim marker stays on the nose axis. The seeker's aim point and the acquire ring do not move with it. As decided in this round the assist was enabled on touch devices only — `GameCoordinator.updateGunAim` passed `GameConfig.isMobile` as its on / off flag — and a device without it fired along the nose (see the update under Consequences).

## Alternatives considered

The plan records the diagnosis and the outcome rather than a comparison of options, so this list is limited to what the record shows: designs that existed or were built and then replaced, and options the round explicitly ruled out or left open.

- **Lock as a mode (the previous flow).** Replaced rather than repaired. Moving its ring to the nose point would have removed the measured failure, but the other findings — progress lost after one frame outside the ring, a launch the player could not call off, nothing on screen until the key was pressed — belong to the mode itself.
- **Launch on lock.** Dropped: a missile leaves only on a press. Holding the key through a lock in progress still fires when it completes, which keeps a hold-and-wait habit working.
- **A ring size per layout.** The old ring was 22% of the short side across on desktop and 18% or 16% on the touch layouts, capped at 180 / 160 px. One rule replaced them (radius 13% of the short side), so touch does not get a smaller ring than desktop.
- **Snap the bullets, leave the cross.** The first aim assist (`659fdf3`) sent the bullets to the lead point whenever it was within 2.5° and 480 m, all or nothing, with the cross left on the nose. The owner asked for the cross to shift with the assist; `214fcd1` made the pull continuous and bounded and moved the cross with the bullets.
- **Cross at a fixed 600 m along the assisted direction.** Built in `214fcd1`. In the third-person view the camera is not at the muzzle, so points at different ranges along one direction do not coincide on screen and the cross did not land on the target; `db9bb72` draws it at the range of the aim point (not nearer than 100 m).
- **Seeker ring following the assisted cross.** Ruled out: the ring and the seeker's aim point stay on the nose axis, so a missile lock depends only on where the aircraft points.
- **The assisted model for keys as well.** Ruled out: keyboard flight keeps the manual model, and `6384e8f` reports an identical attitude against the old code over a 6,000-step comparison.
- **A gun aim assist for the keyboard.** Left open when the round closed, then added in the following round with a smaller cone; see the last consequence.

## Consequences

- In the retest at 1180×820 a lock completes about one second after the nose is put on a target, in both views, and a tap launches. The retests ran in a browser at iPad size with touch emulation, not on a physical iPad.
- The gun cross is where the bullets go and the ring is where the seeker looks, in both camera modes. In the third-person view that point is not the centre of the screen; in a hard pull the nose axis can leave the screen, and the reticle is then not drawn. `LockOnIndicator` publishes the ring radius as `--hud-aim-r` so that the HUD's centre lanes can keep clear of the ring.
- A lock survives a brief excursion, and the cost is slack: a completed lock is held on a target up to 1.6 ring radii from the reticle, and a target outside that is still tracked for half a second.
- The seeker works whenever a missile is aboard, so the tracking brackets and the lock tone appear without the player asking for them. The coordinator rebuilds the full candidate list on every simulation step and the seeker walks all of it, with scratch vectors rather than allocations.
- Launching is the player's call again. Because the lock is kept after a launch, further presses send more missiles to the same target, 0.35 s apart at the least.
- There are two flight models, chosen per simulation step by the input in use. On the stick the player cannot roll freely, fly inverted or pull through the vertical: bank belongs to the model and pitch stops at 75°. Keyboard flight keeps all three axes. On a tablet with a keyboard attached, pressing a steering key switches that step to the manual model. A caller that fills in only the boolean directions gets the manual model, because `flightAssist` is optional and counts as off.
- The gun assist is small by construction: it never shifts the aim more than 2.5° and is gone by 5°, so the nose still has to be flown onto the target, and because the cross moves with the bullets the player sees what the assist is doing. While it pulls, the gun line and the missile aim point differ by up to 2.5°; the dim nose marker and the unmoved ring show which is which. The base spread also narrows from 3° to 1.2° as the assist takes hold (`PlayerSystem`).
- As merged in `601def1`, the gun assist followed the device, not the input in use: `GameConfig.isMobile` is decided once at start-up, so a touch device flown from an attached keyboard still got the assist while it flew the manual model, and desktop bullets went along the nose.
- A keyboard version of the gun assist was an open question when the round closed. _Update (2026-10-10, `b633e97`):_ the owner decided to add a lighter one, and keyboard play now gets the same assist with a cone half the size — full inside 1.25° (`GUN_ASSIST.KEYBOARD_FULL_ANGLE`) and gone by 2.5° (`KEYBOARD_OUTER_ANGLE`) — while touch keeps 2.5° / 5°. The keyboard cone is tighter because that is what was decided: the plan records the owner's choice as a lighter gun-cross assist for the keyboard, with no further reasoning, and half the touch cone is how that was built. The context is the one above — the touch assist answers the iPad report about the thumb stick, and keyboard flight was not part of that report. In effect the keyboard version acts only when a target is almost dead ahead and can shift the aim by 1.25° at most. Everything else is shared — the 480 m limit, the easing, the narrower spread, the cross that moves with the bullets, the nose marker and the unmoved seeker ring. In the code the assist is always on and the solver takes a cone instead (`GunAssistCone = 'touch' | 'keyboard'`); `GameCoordinator.updateGunAim` picks it from `GameConfig.isMobile`, so the choice still follows the device and not the input in use: a touch device flown from an attached keyboard gets the touch cone.
